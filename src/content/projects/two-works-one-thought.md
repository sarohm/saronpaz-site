A series of Hebrew posts on Facebook. Each post sets two of my works side by side, sometimes twenty years apart, and follows the one thought that connects them. It's a journey into my itch to create provoking experiences: between me and others, and between people and themselves. Experiences that make you stop for a moment and wonder, burn in a question, tattoo a memory, connect souls.

### #1 A perfect circle and Leap of Faith

<div class="side">
<img src="/images/two-works/circle-tattoo.webp" alt="A freehand circle tattoo" />
<a href="/portfolio/leapoffaith/"><img src="/images/leapoffaith/001.webp" alt="LeapOfFaith" /></a>
</div>

I asked for a tattoo of a perfect circle, drawn freehand. The tattooist hesitated and asked if I knew it wouldn't come out perfect. I knew. He sweated with concentration, and in the end we both had a cathartic experience and an intimate conversation about life, and about its whole parts and its flawed ones.

It reminded me of the engagement ring I made many years ago: a diamond set in gold and covered completely by it. I called it [Leap of Faith](/portfolio/leapoffaith/), and asked my wife-to-be to put her soul in her palm (literally) and believe in me, and maybe a little in us.

### #2 StoryStore and PièceDeRésistance

<div class="side">
<a href="/portfolio/storystore/"><img src="/images/storystore/001.webp" alt="StoryStore" /></a>
<a href="/portfolio/piecederesistance/"><img src="/images/piecederesistance/001.webp" alt="PièceDeRésistance" /></a>
</div>

An experience is a matter of a moment, a collection of sensory input along a timeline. But the stories we form after it, with a beginning, a middle and an end, are the memory we carry with us. From them we build our identity and the way we choose to act in the world.

In [StoryStore](/portfolio/storystore/), a shop I opened at Dutch Design Week in Eindhoven in 2007, makers hid an object inside a box and sold it to passers-by through the story behind it alone. In [PièceDeRésistance](/portfolio/piecederesistance/), which I curated at Mediamatic in Amsterdam in 2011 around the Arab Spring, people from all over the world sent objects with stories of resistance behind them, and together we built a folk museum of objects and stories.

Both came back to me at an artists' meeting for a community art project in Lod, initiated by [Zumu](https://zumu.org.il/home/). Lod is a Rashomon of stories from every corner of Israeli society, at a time when the stories around us are getting simpler, flatter and more polarized than ever.

### #3 InterCourse and DoppelGänger

<div class="side">
<a href="/portfolio/intercourse/"><img src="/images/intercourse/001.webp" alt="InterCourse" /></a>
<a href="/portfolio/doppelganger/"><img src="/images/doppelganger/001.webp" alt="DoppelGänger" /></a>
</div>

Encounters have always interested me: not (only) the participants, but (mainly) the encounter itself. Martin Buber's Das Zwischen, the space of the relationship, the in-between. Until now the most meaningful encounters in our lives were with other humans. In the coming years a good part of them will be with AI entities.

Twenty years apart: [InterCourse](/portfolio/intercourse/), a series of plates shown at the Ceramics Biennale at the Eretz Israel Museum in 2006, an arena for encounters between people and food. And [DoppelGänger](/portfolio/doppelganger/) from 2017, an encounter between humans and robots through a distorted mirror that gives the robots a layer of character but leaves them dependent on human movement for their form (Science Gallery Dublin, with Zvika Markfeld and partners).

In 2026 we face intelligent entities that can reflect on themselves, express an opinion and a will, and soon will have a body and a presence in space. These are the questions I am exploring these days, in action research, installations and workshops for building and designing entities.
